When new shingles don't match the old ones.

What Tennessee says about a uniform-looking roof after a claim.

The problem

A storm damages part of your roof. Your shingle is discontinued or the old ones have faded, and new shingles won't match. Will your roof end up looking patched?

What Tennessee says

Tennessee's insurance rules say that when replaced items don't match in quality, color or size, the insurer shall replace items so the result has a reasonably uniform appearance, under your policy's terms. For a lot of homeowners, that's the difference between a patch and a roof that looks right.

What we do about it

  • Photograph your existing shingles up close and from the street
  • Identify the brand and color when we can, and whether it's still made
  • Put it all in your written scope so you can show the adjuster

Your insurer decides what's covered. We make sure they're looking at the full picture.

The law in Tennessee

  • Department of Commerce and Insurance Rule 0780-01-05-.10(1)(b), the standards for settling replacement-cost property claims. Read the rule
  • The rule is written for replacement-cost coverage and talks about items and a reasonably uniform appearance. It doesn't say "whole roof." Your policy terms matter.
  • One Tennessee law firm's reading: "Insurers Have to Pay to Match" (2017)

Common questions

Does matching apply to repairs?

The rule is written about replacement under replacement-cost policies. Ask your insurer in writing how they apply it to your claim.

Who decides whether shingles match?

The adjuster decides first. If you disagree, ask for the reasoning in writing and send photos and a shingle sample.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Check your own policy.
