What Tennessee says about matching.

This page covers a Department of Commerce and Insurance rule. It is not a statute, and we are careful not to say more than the rule says.

The problem

A storm damages part of your roof. The shingle is discontinued, or the old shingles have faded, and new ones will not match. Does the insurer have to make it look uniform?

What the rule says

The source is Tennessee Rule 0780-01-05-.10, from the Department of Commerce and Insurance. Its title is "Standards for Prompt, Fair and Equitable Settlements Applicable to Fire and Extended Coverage Type Policies with Replacement Cost Coverage." Subsection (1)(b) says that when a loss requires replacement of items and the replaced items do not match in quality, color or size, the insurer shall replace items so as to conform to a "reasonably uniform appearance," according to the applicable policy provisions. It adds that this applies to interior and exterior losses, and that the insured shall not bear any cost over the applicable deductible.

You can read the rule text on the Cornell Legal Information Institute page for Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0780-01-05-.10.

What a Tennessee law firm says it means

In an August 11, 2017 article, "Tennessee Commissioner of Insurance Confirms: Insurers Have to Pay to Match," attorney Brandon McWherter writes that the 2017 rule confirms insurers must pay for matching, and reads it to mean that if replacement parts do not match, the whole of the part must be replaced, at no extra cost beyond the deductible. The article is at msb.law. That is the firm's reading of the rule, and a law firm that represents policyholders has a point of view.

What the rule does not say

  • It does not use the words "whole roof" or "shingle." It talks about items and a reasonably uniform appearance.
  • It is tied to policy provisions, so your own policy matters.
  • It is written for replacement-cost coverage.
  • It is about replacement. A simple repair is a different question.

What we have and have not confirmed

We read the rule text above. We have not confirmed any separate Tennessee statute on matching, so we do not cite one. Some websites cite a statute number for this. We could not verify that number in the code, so we leave it out. We also did not find a separate public statement from the Department beyond the rule itself. If you need certainty on your claim, ask the Department or a Tennessee attorney.

What we do on a roof

When a replacement may raise a matching question, we photograph the existing shingles close up and at a distance, note the brand and color where we can identify them, and note whether the product is still made. We give that to you in the written scope. You show it to the adjuster. We do not decide whether your claim qualifies, and we do not tell the insurer what to pay. Common questions

Does Tennessee require the insurer to replace the whole roof if shingles do not match?

The rule says that when replaced items do not match in quality, color or size, the insurer shall replace items to conform to a reasonably uniform appearance, according to the policy provisions. It does not say "whole roof." How that applies to your roof is decided case by case.

Does the matching rule apply to repairs?

The rule is written about replacement of items under replacement-cost policies. Industry commentary reads it as not covering simple repairs. Ask your insurer in writing.

Who decides whether my shingles match?

The insurer's adjuster decides first. If you disagree, you can ask for the reasoning in writing, send your photos and a shingle sample, and take the question to the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance or a lawyer.

Does an ACV-only policy get matching?

The rule's title refers to policies with replacement cost coverage. If your policy is ACV only, ask your insurer and, if needed, a lawyer.

This guide is plain-language information, not legal advice. Policies and facts differ, so check your own policy and the statutes linked above.
