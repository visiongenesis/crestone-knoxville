How a roof claim works in Knox County.

The homeowner's steps from damage to a paid roof, and the parts a contractor can and cannot do.

The short version

You file the claim and you own it. Your insurer decides what is covered and what it pays. A good contractor helps you with evidence: an inspection, photographs, a written scope, and a person at the adjuster visit who can point at the damage. That is the role we play. We do not file your claim for you, and we do not act as a public adjuster.

Step 1: Protect the house and take photos

If the roof is leaking, get a temporary tarp or seal on it, and keep every receipt. Photograph the damage before anything is moved or repaired.

Step 2: Read your policy

Look for three things. First, the deductible, and whether storm deductibles are a flat amount or a percentage of the home's value. Second, whether roof payments are on replacement cost value (RCV) or actual cash value (ACV). Third, the deadlines: how long you have to report a loss and to sue. If you cannot find them, ask your agent.

Step 3: Get an inspection

Before you file, a free inspection tells you whether the damage is real and how big it is. We inspect, photograph everything, and write a scope of the damage that you can give your insurer. It does not obligate you to hire us or file a claim.

Step 4: File the claim

You call your insurer or use its app. You get a claim number and an adjuster. Write down the date and the adjuster's name. Ask in writing when to expect a decision. Timelines vary by policy and insurer, so we do not promise dates.

Step 5: Meet the adjuster

The adjuster inspects the roof. We attend with you, walk the roof, and show what we photographed. The adjuster makes the call. Ask for the findings in writing.

Step 6: The decision and the checks

If the claim is covered you get an estimate and payments. On an RCV policy, the first check is usually ACV: replacement cost minus depreciation, minus your deductible. The held-back depreciation is released after the work is finished and you send the insurer proof, such as the final invoice and photos. On an ACV-only policy, the depreciation is not paid back.

If part of the claim is denied, ask for the reasons in writing. You can ask for a re-inspection with evidence. For matching questions on replacements, see our matching guide.

Step 7: The contract, the deposit, and the cancellation right

Tennessee has a short statute for storm-claim roofing contracts. The statute, read on FindLaw on October 6, 2026, says:

  • Before the contract, the roofing provider must give you a written statement with its contact information for cancellations, license or registration numbers, and a detailed description of the damage or repairs the contract covers (Tenn. Code Ann. 62-6-603).
  • You can cancel by signed, dated written notice any time before midnight on the third business day after your insurer tells you that all or part of the claim is not a covered loss (62-6-603).
  • The provider cannot require payment until you have the insurer's written notice that the contract or claim is fully covered, or the three-day cancellation period has run. If you cancel, payments come back within ten business days. Emergency repairs you acknowledged in writing are an exception (62-6-604).
  • A roofing provider cannot act or hold itself out as a public adjuster unless it holds a separate public adjuster license (62-6-605).

Step 8: The work and the deductible

Once you sign, the crew is scheduled. In the City of Knoxville the re-roof needs a permit, which typically takes one to three business days, and the city inspects after tear-off. When the work is done, we walk it with you and send final photos, which you then send to the insurer for the depreciation release.

You pay the deductible. A contractor who offers to waive it, cover it, or send you a rebate is a warning sign. We will not offer it.

Common questions

Who files the roof claim, me or the contractor?

You do. It is your policy and your claim. A roofing contractor can inspect, photograph, and give you a written scope of the damage, but in Tennessee a residential roofing provider cannot act or hold out as a public adjuster without a separate license (Tenn. Code Ann. 62-6-605).

What is the difference between ACV and RCV?

ACV is actual cash value: replacement cost minus depreciation. RCV is replacement cost value. Many policies pay ACV first and release the held-back depreciation after the work is done and documented. Your policy says which kind you have.

Should I pay my deductible to the contractor?

You owe the deductible under your policy. A contractor who offers to waive it, absorb it, or rebate it to you is a warning sign. Tennessee sources treat promises like that as improper, and they can put you in the middle of a misrepresentation to your insurer.

Can I cancel a roofing contract if the insurer denies the claim?

Tennessee gives you a right to cancel a storm-claim roofing contract until midnight of the third business day after your insurer tells you that all or part of the claim is not a covered loss (Tenn. Code Ann. 62-6-603). Read the statute and your contract.

This guide is plain-language information, not legal advice. Policies and facts differ, so check your own policy and the statutes linked above.
