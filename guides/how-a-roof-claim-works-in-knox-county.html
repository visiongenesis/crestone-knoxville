How a roof claim works, start to finish.

Eight steps from storm damage to a paid roof, and exactly where we help.

The short version

You file the claim and it stays yours. Your insurer decides what's covered. We bring the proof: a free inspection, photos of every hit, a written scope, and a person on the roof when the adjuster comes.

1. Protect the house

If water's coming in, get a tarp on it and keep the receipt. Take photos before anything gets moved.

2. Check your policy

Find three things: your deductible (flat or a percent of home value), whether the roof pays replacement cost (RCV) or actual cash value (ACV), and your deadline to report the damage.

3. Get a free inspection

Before you file, know whether the damage is real. We inspect, photograph and write it up. You're under no obligation.

4. File the claim

Call your insurer or use the app. Write down your claim number and your adjuster's name.

5. Meet the adjuster

We'll be there with you, walk the roof, and show what we found. The adjuster makes the call. Ask for the findings in writing.

6. Understand the checks

On most RCV policies, the first check is the actual cash value: replacement cost, minus depreciation, minus your deductible. The held-back depreciation gets released after the roof is done and you send the final invoice and photos. We send you those photos the day we finish.

7. Sign, build, done

Once you sign, we schedule the crew. Most roofs are done in a day. We walk it with you and send final photos.

8. Pay your deductible

It's yours to pay. A contractor who offers to waive or cover it is putting your claim at risk. We never do.

The law in Tennessee

  • Before a storm-claim roofing contract, the roofer must give you a written statement with contact details for cancelling, license or registration numbers, and a description of the work. You can cancel in writing before midnight of the third business day after your insurer says all or part of the claim isn't covered. Tenn. Code Ann. 62-6-603
  • The roofer can't require payment until the insurer confirms coverage in writing or the cancel period has passed, and must refund within ten business days if you cancel. Acknowledged emergency repairs are the exception. 62-6-604
  • A roofer can't act as a public adjuster without a separate public adjuster license. 62-6-605

Common questions

Who files the claim, me or the roofer?

You do. It's your policy. We document the damage and meet the adjuster.

What's the difference between ACV and RCV?

ACV is replacement cost minus depreciation. RCV pays full replacement cost, usually in two checks: ACV first, then the depreciation after the work is done.

How fast do I need to act?

Check your policy for the reporting deadline, and get the inspection soon after the storm while damage is fresh.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Check your own policy.
