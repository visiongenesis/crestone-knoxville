When replacement shingles don't match your roof

Ryan Dix, Crestone Roofing · Last reviewed October 26, 2026

Replacement shingles often don't match because the color was discontinued, the old shingles faded, or the batch is different. Your options depend on your policy and on how visible the patch will be, so ask your insurer in writing and ask your roofer for the closest match before anything is nailed down.

Why patches don't match

  • Discontinued colors. Manufacturers retire colors and whole shingle lines every few years.
  • Fading. A shingle that has sat in the sun for ten years is a different shade from the same color fresh off the pallet.
  • Production runs. Even the same color from a different batch can look a shade off.

What to ask your insurer

Ask whether your policy covers a uniform appearance when repaired areas won't match, and ask them to put the answer in writing. Policies differ, and a written answer is the one you can rely on. Send them photos of the patch next to the old roof.

What to ask your roofer

  • The manufacturer, line and color name of the shingles on your roof now.
  • Which current color comes closest.
  • A photo of a test patch, or a sample held against the roof, before the repair goes on.

What we do

For a repair, we bring the closest match we can find and show you a photo before we nail it down. If it won't blend on a slope people see from the street, we'll tell you, and help you put the question to your insurer. This guide doesn't cite a Tennessee rule on matching, because we haven't confirmed one.

Related questions

What if replacement shingles don't match the rest of my roof?

It happens a lot, because colors get discontinued and old shingles fade. For a repair, we bring the closest match we can find and show you a photo before we nail it down. After a storm claim, ask your insurer in writing how your policy treats a roof that won't match. Our shingle matching guide goes through your options.

Will my insurance pay for a replacement roof?

It depends on your policy and on what caused the damage. Insurance generally covers sudden damage, such as hail or wind. It usually leaves out wear and age. Your insurer makes that call. What we can do is give your adjuster clear, dated photos of every damaged area and a written scope, so the decision is made on good evidence.

What if my claim is denied or only partly paid?

Ask your insurer to put the reasons in writing. Then compare them to the photos. If something was missed, we can re-inspect, take more photos of the specific areas, and help you understand what a re-inspection request should include. Sometimes the denial holds up, and a repair is the right next step.
