Storm damage and insurance roofs.

The claim is yours. We inspect, photograph, write the scope, and stand next to you at the adjuster visit.

What it is

Hail, wind, and fallen limbs damage roofs in Knox County. It is not Colorado hail country, but it happens: hail up to 1.5 inches fell in parts of Knox County on September 4, 2025, according to WBIR. When a covered storm damages your roof, your homeowner's policy may pay for repair or replacement.

When you need us

After a storm, if you see missing shingles, dents in vents or gutters, granules in the downspouts, or a new leak. Damage is often invisible from the ground.

How it works, and who does what

You file the claim with your insurer. It is your claim and your policy. We do not file it, and we do not act as a public adjuster. Tennessee law says a residential roofing provider cannot act or hold out as one without a separate public adjuster license (Tenn. Code Ann. 62-6-605).

  1. We inspect the roof, the attic side when we can get to it, and the gutters.
  2. We photograph what we find and show you every picture.
  3. You get a written estimate that says what is included.
  4. If you say yes, we put a crew on the schedule and give you the date.
  5. When the work is done, we walk it with you and send final photos.

On insurance jobs, our part is the inspection, the photos, a written scope of the damage, and being at the adjuster meeting with you to point at what we found. What the insurer pays is between you and the insurer. The full sequence is in how a roof claim works in Knox County.

Deductible

You pay your deductible. A contractor who offers to waive it or rebate it to you is a warning sign. We will not do that.

What it costs

If the claim is approved, the insurer pays for covered work up to your policy terms, less your deductible. A full replacement on an asphalt-shingle home typically runs $10,000 to $18,000 before insurance. See the replacement page for what moves that number.

Common questions

Will you file my claim for me?

No. You file it with your insurer. We give you the photos and the written scope to support it.

Do I have to pay anything up front?

Tennessee law limits what a roofing provider can require before coverage is confirmed on a storm-claim contract (Tenn. Code Ann. 62-6-604). Read the section yourself and ask any contractor to show you the contract language.

Can you promise the insurer will approve it?

No one can. The insurer decides coverage. We document what is on the roof as accurately as we can.
