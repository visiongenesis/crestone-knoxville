Roofing in Maryville.

Older homes near downtown, newer subdivisions toward the foothills, and more open wind exposure than Knoxville proper.

The houses

Maryville is the Blount County seat, and the housing runs wide. Near downtown and the college there are bungalows and brick houses from the early and mid 1900s. Out along the highways and toward the foothills of the Smokies there are 1990s and 2000s subdivisions, plus newer builds still going up. Older roofs often have more than one layer of shingles and flashing that has been patched over the years. Newer ones are mostly simple architectural shingle roofs.

A roof that is several decades old needs a close look at the decking before anyone quotes it. We check that during the inspection.

Weather

Maryville sits closer to the Smokies than Knoxville does, and storms coming off the mountains can arrive with strong straight-line wind. Open lots and hillside homes take the most. Wind lifts or tears shingles along edges and ridges, and falling limbs do the rest. Hail happens now and then. We don't treat it as the normal case.

What we do and what it costs

We inspect, photograph, and give you a written estimate. We tell you if a repair will do. A typical asphalt shingle re-roof in our region runs $10,000 to $18,000, and metal or steep roofs cost more. Our Tennessee licensing is still in progress; the About page says where it stands.

Permits

Blount County and the City of Maryville each run their own codes office, and the rules for a re-roof can differ from Knoxville's. We find out which office covers your address and handle the permit.

Questions people ask here

Do you work outside the city limits?

Yes. Maryville, Alcoa and the surrounding Blount County are inside our service area. Longer drives can affect scheduling, not the quality of the work.

Wind took off a few shingles. Is that an emergency?

Missing shingles let water in on the next rain, so tarping or a quick repair is worth doing soon. We'll tell you what is urgent and what can wait.

My roof has two layers. Does that change the job?

Yes. Layers are torn off before new shingles go on, which adds labor and disposal. The estimate shows it as its own line.
