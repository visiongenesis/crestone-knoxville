Roofing in Knoxville.

Free roof inspections across the city, from older in-town neighborhoods to the subdivisions out west.

The houses

Knoxville has the oldest housing in our service area. North Knoxville, Fountain City, Sequoyah Hills and parts of South Knoxville are full of bungalows, Cape Cods and brick ranches built from the 1920s through the 1960s. Many of these roofs have been replaced more than once. Older houses often turn up rotted decking under the shingles and flashing that has worn out around brick chimneys.

West and southwest of downtown, the 1970s and 1980s subdivisions have simpler gable roofs. Those are quicker to inspect and easier to price.

What the weather does to roofs here

In Knoxville, wind, heavy rain and falling limbs are the damage we expect to see most. Older neighborhoods have mature hardwoods hanging over the houses, and a limb through a few rows of shingles after a thunderstorm is a normal kind of repair. Hail shows up some years and not others, so we look for it but we don't assume it.

What we do

We inspect the roof, photograph what we find, and send a written estimate. If a repair will do the job, we say so. If the roof is at the end of its life, we show you why.

A typical asphalt shingle re-roof in Knoxville runs $10,000 to $18,000. Metal, steep pitches and roofs with a lot of valleys cost more. Your written estimate is the number that counts. Our Tennessee licensing is still in progress; the About page says where it stands.

Permits

A re-roof inside the City of Knoxville needs a building permit, and so does one in Knox County outside the city limits. We handle the permit for you. The city typically turns an application around in one to three business days, and it inspects after the old roof is torn off and before the new one goes down.

Questions people ask here

Do I have to be home for the inspection?

No. We need access to the yard, and to the attic if you'll allow it. We send the photos afterward and walk you through them by phone or in person.

My roof only has one leak. Do I need a whole new roof?

Not always. A single leak often comes from flashing, a vent boot or a few damaged shingles. We tell you which one it is and price the repair separately from a replacement.

Do you work in the older in-town neighborhoods?

Yes. Tight lots, mature trees and older decking change how a job is planned, and we price for them up front.
