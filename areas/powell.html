Roofing in Powell.

Ranch houses and family subdivisions north of the city, built over several decades and reaching re-roof age at different times.

The houses

Powell is north of Knoxville along I-75, and the housing is mostly single-family: brick and vinyl ranches from the 1960s and 1970s, split-levels and two-story homes in subdivisions from the 1980s and 1990s, and newer streets still being added. Powell is unincorporated, so most of it falls under Knox County rather than a city.

Because the houses were built across so many years, roofs here are at every stage. One street can have a roof from 2005, one from last year and one that is on its third layer.

Weather

Wind and falling limbs cause most of the damage. Trees are large in the older neighborhoods and along the creeks. Wind pulls shingles at ridges and edges, and a leak can start long before a stain shows on the ceiling. Hail happens in some years but is not routine.

What we do and what it costs

We inspect, photograph and send a written estimate. We tell you if a repair will be enough. A typical asphalt shingle re-roof runs $10,000 to $18,000, and many Powell ranches fall in the lower half of that range, since they are single-story with simple rooflines. Our Tennessee licensing is still in progress; the About page says where it stands.

Permits

A re-roof in Knox County needs a permit. We handle it from application to inspection, so you do not have to go to the codes office.

Questions people ask here

How do I know if I need a repair or a replacement?

We look at how widespread the damage is, how old the shingles are and what the decking looks like. A few bad spots get a repair quote. Wear across the whole roof gets a replacement quote.

My roof is only about 20 years old. Is that too soon?

Not necessarily. Sun, heat and poor ventilation can wear shingles out faster than the label says. We check the condition rather than the date.

Do you work in Halls and Corryton too?

We cover the northern parts of Knox County. Tell us your address and we'll confirm.
